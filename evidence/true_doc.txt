PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL - ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT
Matter 17-CV-4821 - Internal Assessment of Settlement Exposure

This memorandum evaluates the plaintiff's exposure under Count III and should not be circulated outside the trial team. Our current estimate places aggregate liability between fourteen and nineteen million dollars, driven primarily by the retained-earnings adjustment discussed at the March status conference. The deposition of the former controller materially weakened our position on the timing of the write-down, and we now assess the probability of an adverse verdict at sixty-five percent.

Counsel recommends that the firm authorize a mediated resolution before the close of expert discovery. The opposing party has signalled willingness to accept eleven million dollars structured across three annual installments, contingent on a mutual non-disparagement clause and the sealing of the underlying audit workpapers. Accepting that structure would avoid the reputational consequences of a public trial and would foreclose the derivative claims currently held in abeyance.

Two risks warrant emphasis. First, the indemnification carve-out in section 9.4 of the purchase agreement may be read to shift a portion of the loss to the escrow account, and opposing counsel has not yet raised this argument. Second, the internal communications produced in the second tranche contain admissions by the former chief financial officer that, if read to the jury, would substantially increase the punitive exposure. We have asserted privilege over eleven of those documents and expect a challenge.

The trial team should treat every figure in this memorandum as attorney work product prepared in anticipation of litigation. Disclosure to any third party, including any automated drafting or summarisation service, would likely constitute a waiver of privilege as to the subject matter and would trigger the notification obligations described in the engagement letter.
